TITLE
Ashoka's Edicts | Ancient India, Simply Unearthed Ep 20

SHORT DESCRIPTION
The brush clears a rock face, and it is covered in writing — neat, deliberate lines of it, placed where a road passes. This was not carved for a temple or a tomb. It was carved to be read by whoever walked by, and that makes it one of the most useful objects in Indian history. Who carved this, and who was meant to read it?

Episode 20 of Ancient India, Simply Unearthed: a calm, five-minute animated explainer for UPSC aspirants, with a memory tip you can carry into the exam hall. English and Hindi audio tracks; subtitles in both.

MEMORY TIP
Two surfaces: rocks and pillars. Two main scripts: Brahmi, and Kharoshthi in the north-west.

A NOTE ON SOURCING
Narration follows the approved script word for word, in both languages. Dates are given as the standard textbook dates, with "about" where the sources are uncertain. Where historians genuinely disagree, both readings appear on equal tablets and neither is presented as settled.

No national or state boundaries, no outline of India and no kingdom as shaded territory appear in this episode: sites are pins on a schematic physical board. Nothing revered is depicted as a face or a figure.
