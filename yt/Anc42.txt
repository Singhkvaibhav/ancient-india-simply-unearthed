TITLE
Coins and Inscriptions | Ancient India, Simply Unearthed Ep 42

SHORT DESCRIPTION
The brush uncovers a tray of coins, laid out in rows. They are not all the same shape, metal or size, and that is the point: lay them in order and you have a timeline you can hold in your hand. This episode is about the two kinds of evidence this series has leaned on hardest, and why they are worth more than any chronicle. What makes an object better evidence than a chronicle?

Episode 42 of Ancient India, Simply Unearthed: a calm, five-minute animated explainer for UPSC aspirants, with a memory tip you can carry into the exam hall. English and Hindi audio tracks; subtitles in both.

MEMORY TIP
Coins date it; inscriptions place it. Between them they hold up the whole chronology.

A NOTE ON SOURCING
Narration follows the approved script word for word, in both languages. Dates are given as the standard textbook dates, with "about" where the sources are uncertain. Where historians genuinely disagree, both readings appear on equal tablets and neither is presented as settled.

No national or state boundaries, no outline of India and no kingdom as shaded territory appear in this episode: sites are pins on a schematic physical board. Nothing revered is depicted as a face or a figure.
